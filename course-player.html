Solving One-Variable Linear Equations

Mathematics 10 Algebra Q3 — Week 5
KEY CONCEPT

A linear equation is an equation where the variable has an exponent of 1. Our goal is to isolate the variable on one side using inverse operations. The equality property requires that every operation be applied to both sides.

WORKED EXAMPLE — SOLVE FOR x
3x + 5 = 20
STEP 1 — Write the equation

Identify the variable, its coefficient, and the constant term.

3x + 5 = 20
coefficient = 3  |  variable = x  |  constant = 5
STEP 2 — Subtract 5 from both sides (inverse operation)

Apply the Subtraction Property of Equality to remove the constant from the left side.

3x + 5 − 5 = 20 − 5
           3x = 15

Rule: Whatever you subtract from one side, you must subtract from the other.

STEP 3 — Divide both sides by the coefficient (3)

Apply the Division Property of Equality to isolate x.

3x ÷ 3 = 15 ÷ 3
          x = 5

Dividing both sides by 3 leaves x alone on the left side.

STEP 4 — VERIFY YOUR ANSWER

Substitute x = 5 back into the original equation to check.

3(5) + 5 = 20
  15 + 5 = 20
      20 = 20 ✓

Why do we subtract 5 first instead of dividing?
Great question! We follow the order of operations in reverse. The +5 is added last, so we remove it first using its inverse (subtract 5). This isolates the term with x before we divide.
